How to Choose a Builder in Noida: 8 Checks Before You Book

By Ayati Group 7 min read Noida · Greater Noida · Buying

Search for builders in Noida and you get long lists of names, but a list tells you nothing about whether a particular real estate developer will hand over your home on time, at the price you agreed. The checks below do. Each one takes an afternoon or less, and each one can be done before you pay a booking amount.

Ayati Group is a residential real estate developer in Noida, so we have an obvious interest here. That is why this guide sticks to checks you can run yourself on any builder, including us.

1. Check the project on UP RERA

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, most residential projects in Uttar Pradesh must be registered with the UP Real Estate Regulatory Authority before they are advertised or sold. Ask for the project's RERA registration number and look it up on the UP RERA website. The listing shows the promoter, the approved plan, the declared completion date and the progress updates the builder has filed.

Small projects, on land under 500 square metres or with no more than eight homes, do not have to register. Many independent-floor buildings fall in this group. If a project is exempt, the remaining checks on this list matter even more.

2. See the approved building plan

Every building in Noida and Greater Noida needs a plan sanctioned by the development authority for that area. Ask to see the sanctioned plan and compare it with the brochure: the number of floors, the number of homes per floor and the covered area should match. A home that is not on the sanctioned plan can cause problems later with registration, loans and resale.

3. Ask about the land title

Most land in Noida is allotted on long lease by the development authority, so the builder's title usually rests on a lease deed. Ask for the title documents and have a lawyer read them, along with any mortgage on the land. This is the one check where a few thousand rupees of legal fees can save you lakhs.

Which banks have approved the project?

A lender that approves a project for home loans has run its own due diligence on the builder and the specific project, including title and approvals. Ask for the list of approving banks and housing finance companies. A long list from established lenders is a good independent signal. It is not a guarantee, but it is a check someone else has already paid for.

"A list of top builders tells you who is big. These checks tell you whether this builder, on this project, will do what the agreement says."

4. Get the full price in writing

The quoted rate per square foot is rarely the whole price. Ask for one written figure that includes everything you will pay before possession:

  • Basic price and the area it is calculated on (carpet area, which RERA requires for registered projects)
  • Preferential location charges for corner, park-facing or road-facing homes
  • Floor-rise charges
  • Car parking, power backup and club or amenity charges
  • Maintenance deposits and any charges collected at possession

If the builder will not put a total in writing before you book, treat that as the answer.

5. Read the payment plan against the law

For a registered project, RERA does not allow a builder to take more than 10% of the cost as an advance or application fee before signing a written agreement for sale. After that, a construction-linked plan, where each instalment falls due when a stage of the building is complete, keeps your money tied to visible progress.

6. Visit buildings the builder has already delivered

Brochures show finished renders. Completed buildings show finished work. Visit an older project, look at the common areas, the drainage and the quality of finishes after a few years of use, and talk to people who live there. Ask them how close the actual handover was to the promised date.

7. Walk the construction site

For an under-construction home, ask to walk the site itself, not just a sample flat. Check that the work on site matches the stage the builder says it has reached, and that the person showing you around can answer specific questions about the structure.

8. Ask about the completion certificate

Before you take possession, the building should have its completion or occupancy certificate from the development authority. Ask when the builder expects to apply for it, and make possession conditional on it in your agreement where you can.
